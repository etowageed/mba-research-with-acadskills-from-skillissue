## 2. Verified Peer-Reviewed Academic Sources (Harvard Format)

1. **Bakker, A.B. and Demerouti, E. (2017)** 'Job Demands–Resources theory: Taking stock and looking forward', *Journal of Occupational Health Psychology*, 22(3), pp. 273–285. doi: 10.1037/ocp0000056.
   - *Evidence Grade*: Level V (Theoretical/Conceptual Review) | *Role*: Core framework for flexibility as a job resource buffering commuting/role strain.
2. **Barrero, J.M., Bloom, N. and Davis, S.J. (2021)** *Why working from home will stick*, NBER Working Paper No. 28731. doi: 10.3386/w28731.
   - *Evidence Grade*: Level III (Large-scale longitudinal survey & structural model) | *Role*: Explains structural persistence of hybrid work, amenity value, and commute savings.
3. **Bloom, N., Han, R. and Liang, J. (2024)** 'Hybrid working from home improves retention without damaging performance', *Nature*, 630, pp. 920–925. doi: 10.1038/s41586-024-07500-2.
   - *Evidence Grade*: Level II (6-month Randomised Controlled Trial, n = 1,612) | *Role*: Causal benchmark showing 33%–35% reduction in quit rates and null-equivalent performance under structured hybrid schedules.
4. **Chung, H. and van der Lippe, T. (2020)** 'Flexible working, work–life balance, and gender equality: Introduction', *Social Indicators Research*, 151(2), pp. 365–381. doi: 10.1007/s11205-018-2025-x.
   - *Evidence Grade*: Level V (Thematic Synthesis / Special Issue) | *Role*: Analyses flexibility paradoxes, boundary management, and sectoral/demographic inequalities.
5. **Cropanzano, R. and Mitchell, M.S. (2005)** 'Social exchange theory: An interdisciplinary review', *Journal of Management*, 31(6), pp. 874–900. doi: 10.1177/0149206305279602.
   - *Evidence Grade*: Level V (Theoretical Review) | *Role*: Explains reciprocity norms linking employer-provided hybrid autonomy to reduced voluntary turnover.
6. **Felstead, A. and Reuschke, D. (2021)** 'A flash in the pan or a permanent change? The growth of homeworking during the pandemic and its effect on employee productivity in the UK', *Information Technology & People*, 34(7), pp. 1960–1981. doi: 10.1108/ITP-11-2020-0758.
   - *Evidence Grade*: Level III (UK Understanding Society / UKHLS longitudinal analysis) | *Role*: UK-specific empirical baseline on homeworking expansion and self-reported productivity.
7. **Gibbs, M., Mengel, F. and Siemroth, C. (2023)** 'Work from home and productivity: Evidence from personnel and analytics data on information technology professionals', *Journal of Political Economy Microeconomics*, 1(1), pp. 7–41. doi: 10.1086/721803.
   - *Evidence Grade*: Level III (Personnel & digital analytics panel, n > 10,000) | *Role*: Crucial counter-evidence showing 8%–19% output-per-hour decline under *fully remote* pandemic conditions due to coordination/meeting costs.
8. **Ryan, R.M. and Deci, E.L. (2000)** 'Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being', *American Psychologist*, 55(1), pp. 68–78. doi: 10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68.
   - *Evidence Grade*: Level V (Theoretical Framework) | *Role*: Explains autonomy, competence, and relatedness trade-offs in hybrid vs. office-bound work.
9. **Teece, D.J., Pisano, G. and Shuen, A. (1997)** 'Dynamic capabilities and strategic management', *Strategic Management Journal*, 18(7), pp. 509–533. doi: 10.1002/(SICI)1097-0266(199708)18:7<509::AID-SMJ882>3.0.CO;2-Z.
   - *Evidence Grade*: Level V (Strategic Management Theory) | *Role*: Frames hybrid workforce reconfiguration (sensing, seizing, transforming) as an organisational capability.
