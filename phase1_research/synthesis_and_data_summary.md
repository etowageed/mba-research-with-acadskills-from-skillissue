## 2. Thematic Synthesis & Contradiction Resolution
1. **Structural Polarisation (H1)**: Hybrid working in the UK is not a uniform macro-trend; it is structurally bifurcated along task-digitalisability and qualification lines (49% in Section J vs. 4% in Section I; 42% degree-holders vs. 4% no qualifications).
2. **Retention vs. Productivity Paradox (H2 & H3)**:
   - *Retention*: Experimental (Bloom et al., 2024: -33% to -35% quit rates) and UK survey evidence (CIPD, 2023: ~2M job exits linked to inflexibility) converge strongly on hybrid work as a potent retention mechanism, explained by **JD-R** (reduced commute strain) and **SET** (perceived organisational support).
   - *Productivity*: Literature reveals an apparent contradiction between Gibbs et al. (2023) (-8% to -19% output/hour in *fully remote* pandemic IT work) and Bloom et al. (2024) (null-equivalent/slight positive performance in *structured 2-day WFH hybrid* work). Macro-level UK ONS `PRDY` data (+1.4% in 2021 to -0.8% in 2024) demonstrates that hybrid adoption alone does not override aggregate macroeconomic headwinds (energy shocks, inflation, hours-worked expansion), even though high-hybrid Section J outperforms low-hybrid Section I.
3. **Identified Research Gaps**:
   - Most UK studies examine either *employee attitudes* (CIPD, OPN) or *macro productivity* (ONS `PRDY`) in isolation. Few MBA-level syntheses integrate **BICS sectoral hybrid adoption**, **LFS/CIPD retention dynamics**, and **ONS output-per-hour trends** across the full 2021–2024 post-pandemic normalisation window.
   - Costed, sector-contingent implementation roadmaps accounting for the **April 2024 Employment Relations (Flexible Working) Act** (where 49% of employers were unprepared) remain scarce.
