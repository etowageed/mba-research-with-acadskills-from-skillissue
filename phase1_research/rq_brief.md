# Research Question Brief — UEL-SG-7002 Applied Business Project

## 1. Topic Area
Strategic Workforce Flexibility and Organisational Performance: A Quantitative Secondary Data Analysis of Hybrid Working Arrangements, Employee Retention, and Labour Productivity Across UK Industry Sectors (2021–2024).

## 2. Primary Research Question (RQ1)
> **To what extent do variations in hybrid working adoption across contrasting UK industry sectors (2021–2024) relate to differences in employee retention (voluntary turnover/job-to-job moves) and sectoral labour productivity (output per hour worked)?**

## 3. Sub-Questions (Mapped to Report Chapters & Hypotheses)
1. **Sub-RQ1 (Adoption & Structural Divergence):** How did the prevalence and permanence of hybrid working arrangements evolve across knowledge-intensive service sectors (Information & Communication; Financial & Insurance Activities; Professional, Scientific & Technical Activities) versus location-dependent sectors (Manufacturing; Wholesale & Retail Trade; Accommodation & Food Service Activities) in the UK between 2021 and 2024?
2. **Sub-RQ2 (Retention & Turnover Dynamics):** To what extent are higher sectoral rates of hybrid working availability associated with improved employee retention, reduced voluntary turnover intentions, and lower job-to-job attrition across UK sectors (2021–2024)?
3. **Sub-RQ3 (Labour Productivity Relationship):** How does sectoral hybrid working intensity correlate with longitudinal changes in UK labour productivity (GVA per hour worked, index and annual percentage change) across 2021–2024, and where do sectoral divergences emerge?

## 5. SMART Research Aim & Objectives
### Research Aim
To critically evaluate and quantify the relationship between hybrid working arrangements, employee retention, and labour productivity across contrasting UK industry sectors from 2021 to 2024 using authoritative quantitative secondary data, in order to formulate evidence-based, costed strategic workforce recommendations for UK organisational leaders.

### SMART Objectives
- **RO1 (Specific, Measurable, Time-bound):** To map and quantify longitudinal trends (2021–2024) in hybrid working adoption across six contrasting UK SIC 2007 industry sectors using ONS Business Insights and Conditions Survey (BICS) and Opinions and Lifestyle Survey (OPN) datasets.
- **RO2 (Specific, Measurable, Achievable):** To examine the statistical and empirical association between sectoral hybrid working prevalence and employee retention/turnover indicators (job-to-job moves, resignation/attrition rates, unmet flexibility demand) using ONS LFS/APS and CIPD survey data (2021–2024).
- **RO3 (Specific, Measurable, Relevant):** To analyse cross-sectoral differences in UK labour productivity (GVA output per hour worked, ONS `PRDY` / Output per Hour datasets) between high-hybrid and low-hybrid sectors over 2021–2024.
- **RO4 (Specific, Achievable, Relevant):** To synthesise empirical findings through Job Demands-Resources (JD-R) Theory, Social Exchange Theory (SET), Self-Determination Theory (SDT), and Dynamic Capabilities to develop actionable, costed strategic recommendations and implementation KPIs for UK employers.

## 6. Testable Quantitative Hypotheses
- **H1 (Sectoral Adoption Divergence):** Knowledge-intensive UK service sectors (Information & Communication; Professional, Scientific & Technical Activities; Financial & Insurance Activities) exhibit significantly higher and more persistent hybrid working adoption rates (2021–2024) than location-dependent sectors (Manufacturing; Wholesale & Retail Trade; Accommodation & Food Service Activities).
- **H2 (Retention Advantage):** Sectors and organisations offering formalised hybrid working arrangements exhibit lower voluntary turnover intentions and stronger talent retention metrics than rigid, location-bound comparators, mediated by reduced commuting strain and perceived organisational support (JD-R / SET).
- **H3 (Sector-Contingent Productivity Relationship):** Higher sectoral hybrid working intensity is positively associated with post-pandemic labour productivity resilience (output per hour worked, 2021–2024) in digitally enabled knowledge sectors, whereas aggregate UK productivity remains constrained by macroeconomic headwinds and sectoral composition effects.

## 7. Scope Boundaries
- **In Scope:** UK economy (Great Britain / UK SIC 2007 industry sections); 2021–2024 longitudinal window; six focal contrasting sectors (3 high-hybrid: Section J Information & Communication, Section K Financial & Insurance Activities, Section M Professional, Scientific & Technical Activities; 3 low-hybrid: Section C Manufacturing, Section G Wholesale & Retail Trade, Section I Accommodation & Food Service Activities); quantitative secondary datasets (ONS, CIPD, Acas, DBT/BEIS) and peer-reviewed academic literature (2019–2025, plus seminal theory).
- **Out of Scope:** Primary data collection (surveys/interviews); non-UK labour markets (except as theoretical/comparative literature context); pre-2020 historical productivity series beyond brief baseline context; informal/gig-economy-only analyses.
- **Key Assumptions:** ONS SIC 2007 classifications remain consistent across BICS and Labour Productivity datasets; ONS LFS/APS 2023–2024 response-rate reweighting caveats are explicitly controlled for and triangulated with BICS/OPN and CIPD series.
